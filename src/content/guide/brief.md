# The brief

Open it from Projects with **New short**, then **Stick-figure skit**. If the skit belongs to a show, use **New episode** on that show’s row instead. The format, cast, set, and tone from the show are already filled in, and the episode number is the next one.

Nothing on this page records a voice. **Write the skit** is the only spend, and the amount is printed on the button: **up to $0.02**.

## How to start

Three tabs sit above the topic. Pick one before you fill the rest. The notes box under the topic is there for all three.

### Topic

This is the normal way.

1. Type the idea in **What’s the skit about?** One or two sentences. Write the situation, not a treatment. “The meeting where everyone agrees and nobody read the doc” is enough.
2. In the notes box, add anything the writer would otherwise guess: who it is for, a line you already want, a thing to avoid. Two thousand characters is the limit. Leave it empty if the topic is enough.
3. The line under the notes is the promise: you read and edit every line before anything is voiced.

### Paste a script

Use this when the words already exist.

1. Press **Paste a script**.
2. Paste the dialogue. A name, a colon, then the line works:

```
Person A: "You get $10."
Person B: That is not the deal.
```

3. **Who is speaking** lists each name it found. Pick the character who plays them.
4. Pick a **Set**.
5. Press **Open the script**.

Parsing is free. It opens on the script and nothing is voiced. On the script, **Tidy for timing · $0.01** appears only for a pasted script. It smooths the timing and leaves the words. It asks you to confirm.

### From a link

Use this when the idea is a page, not a sentence.

1. Press **From a link**.
2. Paste the address.
3. Press **Read the link · $0.01**. Confirm the question. Reading the page is the spend.
4. Each result has a title and a line. **Use this** puts the title into the topic and switches you back to Topic, so you can set cast and set and then write. **Add to the list** collects titles. The count is a reminder to paste them into a show’s Ideas. It does not create projects by itself.

## Format

Press one. **Writer’s pick** lets the draft choose from the cast and the topic. The sentence under the buttons is the description of the one you pressed.

| Format | Who | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exchange | Two | They trade lines. The last one lands the joke. |
| Interview | Two | The first holds the mic. The second answers. Street is its usual set. |
| Me vs me | One person, two sides | One character argues with a labelled version of themselves. |
| POV monologue | One | They talk to the camera under a POV caption. |
| Text slam | One | Words hit the screen while one face reacts. |
| Explainer | Two | A short hook, a concept, then a twist they say. |
| Family | Two | A kid asks. An adult answers badly. |
| Fable | Two | Dash is sure and leaves. Moss has the last true line. |
| Trio | Three | Three people in one room. One of them lands it. |

The cast row changes with the format. Exchange asks for two. POV monologue and Text slam ask for one. Trio asks for three. Me vs me asks for one character and then two labels.

### Me vs me

Pick one character. Two fields appear: that character **as**, and that character **versus**. They start as “me” and “my brain”. Change the words. Both need a label, or **Write the skit** refuses with “Give both sides a label, like me and my brain.” Viewers have to tell the two sides apart.

## Cast

Press a face to include them. Press again to remove them. The caption tells you how many the format wants: one, two, or “one or two” when you left the format on Writer’s pick.

Each person in the cast needs their own character. You cannot put Milo in twice.

The faces you see are the ones this workspace allows. If someone is missing, they were turned off in Settings. Characters you built under **Your cast** appear after the house cast, with the names you gave them.

If any built character has a full rig, a checkbox appears: **Original characters only.** Tick it when the skit must not use a real person’s likeness, or a character you do not have the rights to.

The house cast, and the voice each one starts with:

| Name | Starting voice | The voice is aimed at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milo | Puck, bright and upbeat | Someone earnest |
| June | Kore, firm and even | Someone deadpan |

| Theo | Charon, deep and steady | Someone eager |

You do not pick the voice on the brief. The owner sets it in Settings, and Approve uses that map. You can hear a sample there.

## Scenes

The page says **One scene for now.** The whole skit plays on one set. Do not look for a way to add a second scene on this page. It is not offered yet. If a draft already has more than one scene, the script shows a heading for each, with **+ scene** and **Drop**. A new brief does not offer that choice yet. The script controls are on [The script](/guide/script).

## Set

Press a room, or press **Writer’s pick** and leave the room to the draft. Hover a card to read what is in the room. The name on the card is the one to use when you talk about it.

| You see | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| Plain | An empty backdrop and a soft spotlight. Memes, text slam, anything with no real place. |
| Living room | Window, picture, bookshelf, plant. They stand. |
| Lounge | A couch. Both sit. |
| Office | A desk. The person on the left sits. The person on the right stands. |
| Sunny park | A bench. Both sit. |
| Street | Daytime, a shopfront. They stand. The usual interview set. |
| Kitchen | Counter, pot, fruit, fridge, window. They stand. |
| Bedroom | A bed, a window, a lamp. Both sit on the bed. |
| Cafe | A menu board, an espresso counter, a pastry case. They stand. |
| Classroom | A chalkboard, a clock, a door. They stand. |
| Meeting room | A whiteboard with a chart, blinds. They stand. |
| Second living room | A TV, a floor lamp, photos. They stand. |
| Autumn park | Orange trees and a path. They stand. |
| Night street | Moon, lamps, a closed shop. They stand. |
| Beach | Sea, sand, a striped umbrella. They stand. |
| Stage | A red curtain and a spotlight. One person to camera, or a big announcement. |

You pick one set, or you leave it to the writer. You do not pick both a single set and a list of sets. With one scene, one card is the whole choice.

## Length

**Any**, or **15s**, **30s**, **45s**, **60s**. This is a target, not a lock. The script page shows “about Ns of 30s” when you asked for 30. A joke that needs eight seconds should stay eight seconds. Do not pad it to fill the target.

## Tone

**Any**, **Deadpan**, **Wholesome**, **Chaotic**, or **Dry**. Any means you did not constrain it.

## The limit, and the button

Under **Write the skit**:

- The orange button is the only way off this page that spends money.
- **Limit for this video** is the ceiling for everything this project will spend, including later rewrites and Approve. Press **change**, type a dollar amount, and click away. If you type more than the workspace allows, the page says “This workspace caps a video at $…”. The default cap is $1.00.
- A limit of zero is refused: “Set a limit for this video.”

If the button fails, the message is printed under it in orange. Fix that thing and press it again. The brief is not thrown away.

Common refusals, and what to change:

| The page says | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Each cast member needs their own name. | Remove the duplicate face. |
| … isn’t available in this workspace. | Pick a character or set that is still on, or ask the owner. |
| That format needs one character. / two characters. | Match the cast to the format. |
| Give both sides a label… | Fill both Me vs me fields. |
| This workspace caps a video at $… | Lower the limit, or ask the owner to raise the cap. |
