# The script

This is the page you live on. The lines are the film. The preview on the right is a silent rehearsal until you approve. The top bar says **Script**.

If the brief saved but the draft never arrived, you see the topic and **The brief is saved. Nothing else has been spent.** Press **Write the skit** (up to $0.02) and wait. The rest of this page appears when a draft exists.

## Read the strip across the top

Left to right:

- A count: how many lines, the set, and a rough length, such as “about 18s of 30s”. The length is estimated until real voices exist.
- **Undo** steps back through your own edits. It is grey when there is nothing to undo.
- **Set**, when the skit has one scene and more than one set is allowed. Changing it here is free. It does not rewrite the lines.
- One menu per character, labelled by who they are. Switching Milo for June on that menu changes who is drawn. It does not rewrite their sentences.
- **Adjust**, with **$0.02**. This asks the writer to fit the existing lines to the cast and set you just changed. Use it after you swap a character or a room and the joke no longer sits right. It is the same kind of spend as Rewrite.

Edits save as you work. **Save** on the bottom right forces it. ⌘S does the same. The status next to it is **Saving…**, **Unsaved**, **Not saved**, or **Saved** plus the time.

## A line

Each card is one beat. The number on the left is the order.

The names in pills are the cast. The filled pill is who says this line. Press the other name to hand it over. The small face on the selected name opens the mood: how that face looks while the line is said.

On the right of the card: **↑**, **↓**, **Copy**, **Delete**.

The big text is the line. Click it and type. A few habits that matter:

- Press Enter where you want a new beat. The words before the cursor stay on this card. The words after become the next card. Shift+Enter does not split.
- Double-click a word to slam it. The word is marked, and a text-slam chip appears. A slam is a word that hits the screen big, on that word. Edit the chip, or press × to remove it.
- **+ Text slam** adds one. It starts as WAIT. Change the letters.
- The sound chip is a sound effect on this line. Pick one, or clear it.
- On the first spoken line only, **Try other hooks · $0.01** asks for other openings. Confirm the question. Pick one of the lines that come back and it replaces the hook. This spends. Editing the hook yourself does not.
- **Pause** is the silence before this line, from 0 to 2 seconds.

**Add a line** under the list appends an empty beat. Give it a speaker and some words, or delete it. An empty spoken line will fail the check.

A draft that already has more than one scene shows a heading, **Scene 2 of 3** and so on. On that heading: the set for that scene, **↑** and **↓** to reorder scenes, **+ scene** to insert one after it, and **Drop** to remove it. A brand-new brief still says one scene. You only see these controls once a draft has scene headings.

Along the bottom of the preview, the small faces are the same beats. Click one to jump the preview there. Drag one onto another to reorder. Reordering on the cards with the arrows does the same thing.

## When the writer just rewrote it

After **Rewrite** or **Adjust**, a bar sits above the lines: **Changed by your note**, then the sentence you sent.

- **Keep** accepts that draft and clears the bar.
- **Undo rewrite** puts the previous draft back.

You get one step of rewrite undo. Your own typing undo is separate, and it is the **Undo** button in the strip.

**Ask for a change** is at the bottom of the lines. Write what you want in ordinary language. The placeholder is a fair example: “make June meaner, or land the punchline sooner.” Press **Rewrite**. It is disabled until the box has text. Cost: up to $0.02. You can undo it, as above.

Do not use Rewrite to fix a typo. Type the typo out yourself. Rewrite spends money and can move lines you were happy with.

## The preview

The phone on the right plays the skit with Stick Stage’s own picture: the characters, the set, the cuts, the captions. Until you approve, there is no recorded voice.

- The line under it says whether sound is on. **Mute** and **Unmute** switch that. Before a film exists, timing is estimated and the track is silent even when sound is on.
- **Hear it** speaks the lines in the browser with the first character’s voice. It is a listen, not the recording Approve will make.
- **Music** picks a bed, or **None**. **Duck** is how far the bed drops under the words. **Save bed** stores that choice on the skit. Picking a bed and forgetting Save bed leaves it as a preview only.
- The chips above those controls are the cuts the director chose: a wide two-shot, a punch-in, a close-up. Click a chip to jump to that frame. Hover one to read why that cut is there.
- **Pin two**, **Pin close**, and **Pin wide** lock the current line to that framing. Press the same one again to let the director choose. Pin close needs someone speaking.
- **No reaction** stops the other person from reacting on this line. Press it again to allow the reaction.
- **−0.3s** and **+0.3s** nudge the pause before the current line. The pause slider on the card is the same control, in finer steps. A pause cannot go past 2 seconds.

## The check

The line above Approve is the self-check. Open it.

| It says | Meaning | Approve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No problems | The draft can be drawn. | Allowed |
| Worth a look | Warnings. Read them. | Allowed |
| 1 problem, or N problems | Something the renderer will refuse. | Blocked |
| writer adjusted N | The writer repaired something on the way in. The notes are in the list. | Depends on the problems |

Each row is **Problem** or **Look**, then where, then the sentence. Click it. The page scrolls to that line.

If you see **Slam timing was corrected. Save to keep it.**, a slam was pointed at a word the line no longer has, and the page moved it. Press **Save**.

If a strip across the top says **This skit was written against an older set of characters, sets and props**, t-shoot has updated its catalog since the skit was written. Press **Move to current catalog**. It's free. The check runs again, and anything the new catalog no longer has shows up in the list as a problem to fix.

The sentence under Approve tells you the same thing in one line: “Nothing is spent until you approve. ⌘S saves.” or “Fix the problem the check found first.” or “The video is being made from the skit you approved.”

## Approve

**Approve** shows the voice cost, about $0.0015 a spoken line, rounded on the button. Twelve lines is about $0.02. The drawing is included.

Press it once.

- **Waiting to start…** then **Voicing the lines and drawing the video…** The dot pulses. Stay on the page or come back. It refreshes itself.
- **Version 1 is ready.** Then **Review** and **Download**. If you edited the script after that film, the line adds **changed the skit since this video**. Approve again to make version 2 from the new lines. The old version stays in the list on Review and Export.
- **The video wasn’t made.** Read the rest of the sentence. Then press **Approve** again after you have fixed the cause. A failed try does not block a new one. Approving the identical script again, after a success, does not start a second job and does not charge again.

While a film is being made, Approve stays disabled. Do not refresh in a panic and press it twice. The second press on the same script is ignored on purpose.

Open [Review](/guide/review) when version 1 is ready.
