# Make your first skit

Do this once, in order. You will end with a vertical video you can download. Plan on reading the script carefully. The writing itself is a few seconds. Drawing the film takes longer than the writing, and the page tells you while it works.

## Before you start

Open the studio and sign in if you are asked.

1. Go to **Sign in**.
2. Type your email and press **Email me a link**.
3. Open the link in that email. It expires in 30 minutes.

Look at the top of the Projects page. A small pill sits near Settings.

- **Tamtree connected** means new skits are written and filmed for real.
- **Tamtree not connected · mock** means you are looking at a sample. The buttons still work, and the script will be a canned joke, not one written from your brief. Ask the person who runs the studio to connect it before you brief a real client. The steps below are the same either way.

## 1. Start a project

On Projects, press **New short**.

You land on **What are you making?**

1. Choose **Stick-figure skit**. The card says “Voice a joke.”
2. You are now on the brief. The question at the top is **What’s the skit about?**

Leave the tabs on **Topic**. The other two tabs are for a script you already have, and for a link. Those are on [The brief](/guide/brief).

## 2. Fill the brief

Type one sentence a person could say out loud. A good first one:

> Replying “sounds good” to a message you didn’t read.

Under that, the smaller box is optional. Use it if the joke has a specific landing, or a person it is for. Skip it on the first try.

Then set the rest, top to bottom:

1. **Format.** Press **Exchange**. Two people trade lines, and the last line is the joke. The line under the buttons explains the format you picked.
2. **Cast.** Press two faces. For a first skit, Milo and June. An orange border means they are in. Press a face again to take them out. Exchange needs two.
3. **Scenes.** It says “One scene for now.” Leave it.
4. **Set.** Press **Office**, or press **Writer’s pick** and let the draft choose the room.
5. **Length.** Press **30s**, or leave **Any**.
6. **Tone.** Press **Deadpan**, or leave **Any**.

The spend line under the button is the most this video is allowed to cost. Leave it unless someone told you a lower number. **change** lets you type one. It cannot go above the workspace cap.

## 3. Ask for the script

Press **Write the skit**. The button says **up to $0.02**. That is the most this click can cost. The label changes to **Writing the skit…**

When it finishes you are on the script: a list of lines on the left, a phone-shaped preview on the right.

If you land on a page that shows your topic and the sentence “The brief is saved. Nothing else has been spent.”, the write did not finish. Press **Write the skit** again. The error under the button is the reason. If it names a character or a set that “isn’t available in this workspace”, the owner turned that choice off in Settings. Pick another, or ask them.

## 4. Read it out loud

Read every line. The first spoken line is the hook. If you would not stop scrolling for it, change it.

On a line:

- Click the text and type. Your change saves on its own. The corner says **Saved** and a time. If it says **Unsaved** or **Not saved**, press **Save**, or press ⌘S.
- Press the other character’s name to give them the line.
- The face next to a selected name is the mood. Open it and pick another.
- **↑** and **↓** move the line. **Copy** duplicates it. **Delete** removes it.
- **Add a line** sits under the list.
- Press Enter in the middle of a line to split it into two lines.

The preview on the right plays with no voices until a film exists. Press play on it. The chips under it are the cuts. Click one to jump there.

Under the preview, open the check line.

- **No problems** in green means you can approve.
- **Worth a look** in amber is a warning. You can still approve. Read it.
- **1 problem** (or more) in orange blocks **Approve**. Click the problem. It scrolls to the line. Fix it, and the count drops.

## 5. Change the joke, if you want

You can keep editing by hand for free.

If you want the writer to take another pass, scroll to **Ask for a change**. Write a plain note, for example “Make June meaner, and land the punchline sooner.” Press **Rewrite**. That is another **up to $0.02**, and you can undo it.

A bar appears: **Changed by your note**, then your sentence. Press **Keep**, or **Undo rewrite** to go back to the previous draft.

## 6. Approve

When the lines are the joke you want, look at the price on **Approve**. It is about $0.0015 for each spoken line, so a 12-line skit is about two cents. The button stays grey while the check has a problem, or while a film is already being made.

Press **Approve**.

The line under the preview changes.

- **Waiting to start…** means it is in the queue.
- **Voicing the lines and drawing the video…** means the voices are being recorded and the film is being drawn. Leave the page open. It updates on its own.
- **Version 1 is ready** means it finished. **Review** and **Download** appear next to it.
- **The video wasn’t made.** means it failed. The rest of that line is the reason. Fix what it names, or tell the person who runs the studio, then press **Approve** again. A failed attempt can be tried again. A successful one is not filmed a second time unless you change the script.

## 7. Watch it, then take it out

Press **Review** in the top bar, or the Review link that appeared.

1. Play the film. The dots on the bar are comments, once any exist.
2. On the right, press **Create a review link** if a client should see it. Press **Copy link** and send it. They need no account. The page they open shows no prices.
3. Or skip the client and press **Export** in the top bar.

On Export:

1. Play the file once more.
2. Press **Download MP4**. That is the video, 1080 by 1920.
3. **Captions** is the subtitle file. **Post text** is a text file with the caption and the script.
4. **Copy caption**, **Copy hashtags**, and **Copy AI-voice line** put those on your clipboard. The voice line says the voices are generated. Use it when you post.

You have a skit. The next pages are the same screens, with every control named, for the second time through and for anyone you hand this to.
