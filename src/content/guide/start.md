# Start here

A stick-figure skit is a short vertical video. Two (or one) big-headed characters stand in a drawn room, say a joke, and the words appear on screen as they talk. You write the idea. The studio writes a draft, you fix the lines, and then it records the voices and draws the film.

Stick Stage is the part that draws it: the characters, the room, the cuts, the mouths, the captions. You do not operate Stick Stage by hand. You work in this studio. Stick Stage runs behind **Approve**.

## The four steps

Every skit goes through the same steps. The bar at the top of a project shows where you are.

1. **Brief.** You say what the joke is, who is in it, and where it happens. This page spends a small amount when you ask for the script.
2. **Script.** You read every line, change what you want, and watch a silent preview. Nothing is recorded yet.
3. **Review.** You send a link. Your client watches the finished film and can comment on a moment, with no account.
4. **Export.** You download the video, copy the caption, and prepare a post.

There is no separate edit timeline for a skit. The script is the edit. If a line is wrong, you change the line.

## What you pay for

The price is on the button before you press it. A click that spends money says so. Typing in a box does not.

| You press | What happens | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write the skit | A draft of the joke, from your brief | up to $0.02 |
| Rewrite, or Adjust | Another pass on the lines | up to $0.02 |
| Try other hooks | Other opening lines for the first spoken line | $0.01 |
| Read the link | Topics pulled from a web page | about $0.01 |
| Approve | Voices recorded, then the film drawn | about $0.0015 per spoken line |
| Save, Undo, moving a line, changing a word | Your own edits | nothing |

Drawing the film is included in the Approve price.

A workspace has a cap per video. It starts at $1.00 unless the owner changed it. A single skit, even with a rewrite, stays well under that. If a button will not go through, the page tells you the cap.

Pressing **Approve** twice on the same script does not make a second film and does not charge twice. Change the lines, then approve again, and you get a new version.

## Who does what

- An **owner** invites people, sets the characters and voices, and sets the spend cap.
- An **editor** makes skits: brief, script, review, export.
- A **client** opens a review link. They watch, comment, and can approve. They do not see prices or settings.

If you are new and you just want one video, go to [Make your first skit](/guide/first-skit) and follow it from the top. Come back to the screen pages when a control is unfamiliar.

If you install the machines, keep the API keys, or have to explain a failed run, read [How the studio is wired](/guide/running-it) after the screen pages. That page is the same path, named as the three programs that carry it.
