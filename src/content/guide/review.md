# Review

Review is how someone who is not in the workspace watches the film and talks about a moment. They get a link. They do not get a login, a price, or a settings page.

Open it from the top bar, **Review**, after a version exists. If you open it earlier, the page says **Share for review** and explains that a version appears once the film has been made. You can still read this page. You cannot show a film that is not there.

## Watch the version you mean to share

The film plays on the left. If there is more than one version, the **Version** menu switches which one you are watching. The caption under the title is the version number and the length.

The thin bar under the player grows a dot for each comment on this version. Press a dot to jump to that time. The time readout is current position, length, and the words “dots are comments”.

## Send the link

On the right, under **Share**:

1. Press **Create a review link**. One link belongs to one version.
2. Press **Copy link**. Send that URL. It looks like your studio address plus `/r/` and a long token. The box on the page shows only the start of the token. The copied value is the full link.
3. **Stop sharing** kills that link. The film is still in the studio. The URL starts failing for the client.

**Approved by** and a name appears on the card once the client presses Approve on their page.

If you change the film and approve a newer version, the old link still points at the old version. The line **Changed something since? Share the latest edits** makes a new link for the latest version. Send the new one. Do not assume the old URL moved.

## What the client sees

They open the link and get the film, a play button, a bar, and two actions. No cost.

1. They play it.
2. To comment, they press **Request changes**, type in **What would you change here?**, and press **Post comment**. The comment sticks to the time the playhead was on.
3. To accept it, they press **Approve**. The button becomes **Approved by** their name. They can still have left comments. Your side will say “Approved, with comments still open.”

Ask them to pause on the moment before they comment. The time is the moment you will jump to.

## Comments waiting on you

**Needs you** is the count of comments that are still open.

Each card shows the person’s name, the time, the version, and the words. Press the time to seek.

Two ways to clear a card:

- **Turn into a change** opens the script with that comment attached, so the next **Rewrite** can use it as the note. Do this when the words should change. For a stick-figure skit this goes to the script, because there is no separate timeline.
- **Mark done** closes it without a rewrite. Use it when you already fixed the thing, or when you are not going to. Marked-done comments fold into **marked done**. Comments you turned into changes fold into **turned into changes**.

**No comments waiting.** means the list is clear. That is not the same as the client having approved.

## Versions

The bottom of the side panel lists every version, its length, and who approved it if anyone did. Press a row to watch that cut, when the file is there.

A version is kept when you share or when the film is made. Deleting a project deletes its versions with it. Stopping a link does not.

## A sensible pass with a client

1. Watch version 1 yourself on this page before you copy the link.
2. Send the link. Tell them Approve means they accept this cut, and Request changes means they want another.
3. When comments land, they also show on **Reviews** in the top bar of Projects, as a count. That page is titled **Waiting on you**.
4. For each comment, either Turn into a change or Mark done. Do not leave them open and also start a quiet rewrite. The client cannot see that you did the work.
5. Change the lines on the script. Approve again. Wait for the new version.
6. Come back here and share the latest. Send the new link. Ask them to approve that one.

Then go to [Export and post](/guide/export).
